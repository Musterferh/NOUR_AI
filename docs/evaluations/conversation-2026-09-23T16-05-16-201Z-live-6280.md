Raw user-facing answer (no application warning appended):

> **Conclusion: No — the company's argument fails. Allocation is not authorisation.**
> 
> The ITU's guidance on National Tables of Frequency Allocations draws the distinction directly: "Allocations are granted to radiocommunications services, while assignments are granted to the operator of a radiocommunication station." [source:primary-itu-spectrum-2015]
> 
> **The distinction:**
> 
> - **Allocation** is a *table-level* decision. It designates a frequency band for one or more radiocommunication services — it answers "what *kind* of service may use this band?" It is a planning framework, not a permission slip.
> - **Assignment** is a *station-level* decision. It is granted to the operator of a specific radiocommunication station, authorising that station to use a frequency or channel under stated conditions.
> 
> **Why the company's reasoning fails:**
> 
> A service allocation answers a categorical question; an assignment answers an individual one. The company's station is not a "service" — it is a particular operator's equipment. Nothing in the allocation grants it transmission rights; it must obtain an assignment, and that assignment must in turn fit within the band's allocation. The two operate at different levels and neither substitutes for the other.
> 
> **Boundary note:** This reasoning establishes the general principle only. It does not identify any current Nigerian band, licensee, or authorisation — those would require evidence from the applicable national instruments, which is not supplied here.
