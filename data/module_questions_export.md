## Module 1: NCA 2003 & Regulatory Framework

### Question 1.1: The 90-Day Statutory Rule on Licensing
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Under Section 41 of the Nigerian Communications Act (NCA) 2003, what happens if the NCC neither grants nor refuses an individual licence application (outside a competitive bidding process) within 90 days? Does regulatory silence constitute deemed approval?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Rule:** Under Section 41(1) & 41(2), NCC must notify the applicant within 90 days of grant, special conditions, or reasons for refusal.
  - **Deemed Refusal:** If NCC fails to act within 90 days, the application is **deemed refused** at the end of the period (unless written approval is issued subsequently).
  - **Exam Trap:** Regulatory silence is **not** deemed approval; it is deemed refusal.

### Question 1.2: Commission Board Composition and Quorum
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Under Section 5 and the First Schedule of the NCA 2003, explain the Board composition, the minimum serving membership requirement, and what constitutes a valid meeting quorum."
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Total Board:** 9 Commissioners appointed by the President, confirmed by the Senate.
  - **Minimum Serving Requirement:** At least 6 serving Commissioners (the Executive Vice-Chairman/CEO, 2 Executive Commissioners, and 3 Non-Executive Commissioners).
  - **Quorum:** A simple majority of members then constituting the Board, which **must include at least 2 Executive Commissioners**.

### Question 1.3: Statutory Objective vs Legal Power
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Why is an institutional objective under Section 1 of the NCA 2003 not a legal substitute for a statutory power? How must an NCC officer justify a regulatory action?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - An **objective** defines the desired outcome/policy goal; a **power** is specific legal authority granted under the Act to execute an action.
  - Good intentions or policy desirability cannot cure an absence of statutory enabling power, due process, or evidentiary basis.

## Module 2: Spectrum Management & Policy

### Question 2.1: Allocation vs Allotment vs Assignment
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Using ITU NTFA guidance and NCC regulatory practice, distinguish between frequency allocation, frequency allotment, and frequency assignment. If a band is allocated to a mobile service, does that give an operator permission to transmit?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Allocation:** Entry in the Table of Frequency Allocations designating a band for use by one or more radio services.
  - **Allotment:** Designation of a radio channel/band for use in a particular geographic country or area.
  - **Assignment:** Authorization of a specific radio station/operator to use a specific frequency channel under defined technical conditions.
  - **Conclusion:** An allocation alone does **not** grant station or transmission permission; an individual assignment/authorisation is required.

### Question 2.2: NFMC vs NCC Spectrum Roles
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Distinguish the role of the National Frequency Management Council (NFMC) from the NCC's role in spectrum administration."
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **NFMC:** Coordinates national trans-sectoral radio frequency allocation and bulk allocation among statutory bodies (Communications/NCC, Broadcast/NBC, Aviation, Defence, Maritime).
  - **NCC:** Administers, assigns, licenses, and monitors spectrum within the telecommunications and commercial communications sector.

### Question 2.3: Spectrum Scarcity and Reusability
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Why is radio frequency spectrum defined as a scarce economic resource if electromagnetic radiation does not physically wear out or permanently deplete?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - Spectrum is scarce in terms of **concurrent, mutually interference-free use** in the same geographical area, frequency band, and time.
  - Multiple transmissions on the same frequency in the same location cause destructive interference; therefore, coordination, licensing, and planning are mandatory.

## Module 3: Quality of Service (QoS) & Consumer Protection (QoE)

### Question 3.1: QoS vs QoE (Technical vs Experiential)
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Critically assess the claim: 'QoS is exclusively technical network performance, so user satisfaction belongs solely to QoE and can never be part of QoS.' How does ITU-T G.1033 Appendix II.1 define this relationship?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **The premise is too narrow.** Under ITU-T G.1033 (Appendix II.1), Quality of Service (QoS) encompasses the totality of characteristics that bear on the ability to satisfy stated and implied needs, including non-technical and customer service aspects.
  - **QoE:** Focuses on user-perceived subjective experience, psychological expectations, and context of use.

### Question 3.2: Redress Sequence and Operator-First Escalation
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Explain why the NCC enforces an 'operator-first' redress sequence for consumer complaints before escalating to the Commission. When can the NCC intervene directly?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Why Operator-First:** The service provider controls the subscriber account, billing engine, and network infrastructure, and is best positioned to resolve issues immediately.
  - **When NCC Intervenes:** When the operator fails to respond within prescribed SLAs, when the consumer remains dissatisfied with the final resolution, when systemic/bulk billing errors occur, or when serious consumer harm or regulatory breach is present.

### Question 3.3: Operative QoS Regulatory Hierarchy

## Module 4: Subscriber Identity, NIN-SIM & TIRMS

### Question 4.1: MSISDN Recycling and Fraud Risks
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Why is the recycling and reassignment of dormant MSISDNs (mobile numbers) treated as a national cybersecurity and financial fraud issue rather than an ordinary administrative inventory action?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - Mobile numbers function as primary identity tokens for banking OTPs, digital financial services (fintech), pensions, and two-factor authentication (2FA).
  - Reassigning a recycled number without purging previous identity and banking linkages exposes the previous subscriber to account takeover and fraud.

### Question 4.2: TIRMS Architecture and Cross-Sectoral Mandate
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "What is the Telecom Industry Risk Management System (TIRMS), which regulatory bodies are involved, and what is its official status in the NCC knowledge baseline?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Purpose:** A centralized, regulatory-backed platform to manage risk associated with churned, recycled, swapped, and blacklisted MSISDNs in near-real-time.
  - **Participating Entities:** NCC (lead), CBN (banks/e-fraud), NIMC (national identity), PENCOM, NAICOM, CAC, and security agencies.
  - **Status:** February 2026 consultation paper is `[CONSULTATION]`; NCC TIRMS API documentation is `[CURRENT]` technical evidence; universal nationwide rollout is `[VERIFY]`.

### Question 4.3: NIN-SIM 2024 Audit Impact on Sector Statistics
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "According to the NCC 2024 Year-End Performance Report, how did the NIN-SIM verification audit affect active voice and internet subscription figures? Did this indicate a contracting telecommunications industry?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Subscription Impact:** Active voice subscriptions fell by ~26.6% (from 224.7M to 164.9M) and internet subscriptions fell by ~15.0% (to 139.3M) due to unlinked SIM deactivations and MNO database cleanup.
  - **Not Contraction:** Data traffic consumed actually grew +34.26% (to 9.76M TB), broadband penetration rose marginally to 44.43%, and CAPEX increased, proving sector usage and capacity were expanding despite fewer active SIMs.

## Module 5: Emerging Technologies & Sector Architecture

### Question 5.1: 5G Spectrum vs Deployment vs Device Capability
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "In Level 10 exam analysis, why must an officer strictly separate a 5G spectrum award, network deployment, population coverage, and device capability?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Spectrum Award / Licence:** Legal right to use 3.5 GHz frequencies; does not mean a single base station has been built.
  - **Deployment:** Physical installation of 5G gNodeBs and fibre backhaul.
  - **Population Coverage:** Geographical reach of the 5G signal (~13.28% in Dec 2024).
  - **Device Capability:** Subscriber possession of compatible 5G handsets. Conflating these leads to faulty regulatory assessment.

### Question 5.2: Direct-to-Device (D2D) Satellite Status
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Under the January 2026 Consultation Paper on Direct-to-Device (D2D) Satellite Services in Nigeria, does this publication authorise commercial satellite transmission to standard mobile handsets?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **No.** The January 2026 document is strictly a `[CONSULTATION]` paper inviting stakeholder views on regulatory frameworks, spectrum coordination, and licensing models.
  - It is **not** an operational licence, not a spectrum assignment, and does not grant commercial transmission authorization.

### Question 5.3: Collocation vs Interconnection
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Define collocation and distinguish it from interconnection. Can two operators collocate without interconnecting, or interconnect without collocating?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Collocation:** Sharing physical site space, towers, power, cooling, and security support.
  - **Interconnection:** Physical and logical linking of networks to allow subscribers on different networks to exchange traffic.
  - **Independence:** Yes. Two MNOs can share the same tower (collocation) without exchanging traffic there; conversely, two networks can interconnect at a designated Point of Interconnection (PoI) without sharing cell site towers.

## Module 6: Institutional Governance & Public Service / Employee Handbook

### Question 6.1: Official Working Hours and Lunch Breaks
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "According to Section 1.4 of the NCC Employee Handbook, what are the official working days, working hours, and lunch break period?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Working Days:** Monday to Friday.
  - **Working Hours:** 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM (08:00 – 17:00).
  - **Lunch Break:** One hour between 12:00 PM (noon) and 2:00 PM (14:00).
  - **Status Note:** Sourced from the provided Handbook text; dynamic operational updates carry `[VERIFY]`.

### Question 6.2: Probation Period and Confirmation Conditions
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Under Employee Handbook sections 2.7 and 2.8, what is the duration of the probationary period, and does an employee become automatically confirmed once that time elapses without a formal letter?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **Duration:** 12 months (one year).
  - **Automatic Confirmation:** **No.** Confirmation is never automatic upon the passage of time.
  - **Conditions:** Requires a satisfactory written performance appraisal report from the supervisor and formal written communication of confirmation issued by Human Capital.

### Question 6.3: Institutional Hierarchy: FMCIDE vs NCC vs USPF
- **Prompt to paste into AI:**
  > "Explain the institutional relationship between the Federal Ministry (FMCIDE), the NCC, and the Universal Service Provision Fund (USPF). Is USPF merely an internal department of the Commission?"
- **Expected Verification Key:**
  - **FMCIDE:** Sets overarching federal telecommunications policy and represents Nigeria internationally at the ministerial level.
  - **NCC:** Independent statutory regulatory authority established under NCA 2003.
  - **USPF:** Created under Part IV of NCA 2003 as a dedicated fund for unserved and underserved areas. It is supervised by the **USP Board**; the USP Secretariat is housed within NCC pursuant to Section 118. It is an independent statutory fund and mechanism, **not** merely an ordinary internal department.
